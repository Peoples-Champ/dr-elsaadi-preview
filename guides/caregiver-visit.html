Helping a parent prepare for a visit—while keeping their voice first

A respectful appointment plan for adult children, partners and other people helping with care.

Prepared for the office of Magdi Elsaadi, MD

Start here

Agree on your role before the visit. Help organize the information while allowing the patient to describe their priorities.

Ask what kind of help is wanted

Before gathering records, ask: “Would you like me to take notes, help remember questions or explain something we have noticed at home?” The person receiving care may want help with one task and privacy for another.

Choose a signal for when they want you to add a detail. A shared plan can keep a helpful companion from unintentionally answering every question. Ask the office how consent and authorized access are handled before requesting private information.

Separate observations from conclusions

Write what happened and when, without assigning a diagnosis. “The morning pills have been left in the organizer several times” is a clearer observation than a broad conclusion about why.

Bring the patient’s own main question to the top of the list. Keep your observations underneath, with dates or examples when possible. If there are more concerns than can fit into one visit, ask how to arrange follow-up.

Prepare a small shared packet

With the patient’s permission, organize the essentials in one place.

  • Current medication information and containers requested by the office.
  • Names of specialists and upcoming appointments.
  • Recent discharge instructions or outside reports the office asked for.
  • A short list of practical barriers: transportation, obtaining prescriptions or understanding written instructions.

Leave with responsibilities, not assumptions

At the end, ask the patient to explain the plan in their own words, if they are comfortable doing so. Offer to write down the next steps and clarify which tasks they want help with.

Do not share a Healow password as a shortcut. Ask the office about approved caregiver access. Established patients use Healow for secure questions; new patients call for their first visit. Dr. Elsaadi speaks English, Spanish and Arabic, and office staff speaks English and Spanish. Mention communication needs when scheduling.

Questions to take with you

  • What does the patient most want addressed today?
  • Who is responsible for each follow-up task?
  • How should authorized caregiver communication work?
